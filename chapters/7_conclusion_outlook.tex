\chapter{Conclusion}\label{chapter:conclusion}

Investigating the performance of Steane-type error correction under circuit level noise we found that the threshold is at reached at a physical noise rate of $3\%$ for single QEC cycles.
The threshold drops off for consecutive repeated independent QEC cycles, reaching a plateau at a $2\%$ after a few rounds. 

Comparing those results to a conventional repeated syndrome extraction circuit, which shows a threshold at $1\%$, 
we find that STEC is able to improve the factor by a factor of $3$ for the single QEC cycles case. 
This improvement drops of to a factor of $2$ for the repeated cycle, due to the lesser influence of the initial data qubit error channel.

We were also able to show that the internal asymmetry of the circuit has an influence on the determined threshold. 
For the single cycle case the difference in the obtained thresholds is approximately $0.5\%$ between the two observables. 
Depending on the operations performed between those QEC cycles one could use such asymmetry advantageously.  
Such asymmetry is suppressed for the repeated cycle case for the quantum memory experiment, as an extended symmetric bulk appears, 
leading to the thresholds of the same value for both observables.

We were also able to find indications of the asymmetry in the residual error, by observing the difference between ML and MWPM decoding of the residual error syndrome.

The whole analysis was performed under the assumption that we have access to fault-tolerant prepared logical encoded qubits, with independent a depolarizing error chanel on each physical qubit. 
Moreover the ML decoder used was only given the error model of either $X$- or $Z$-type errors, 
as such each half-cycle was independently decoded, and the possible information contained in the correlation present in the error channels was neglected.  
While we used the assumption that the conventional syndrome extraction circuit is not decoded independently, 
instead all syndromes, including the syndrome of the residual error, are decoded together. 

\section{Outlook}

Further investigation is needed on the influence of the state preparation for the logical encoded ancilla states.
Modeling or implementing those would lead to a fair comparison between the conventional and the STEC approach to syndrome extraction. 
This would reduce the required assumption of this work. 

A Further point of interest is the implementation of a maximum likelihood decoder that is able to use the correlation between $X$- and $Z$-type errors 
to reach a lower logical error rate. 
Such an decoder is proposed \cite{efficient_decoding_algorithm}, only implementing MLD approximately but still working efficiently.
For such an implementation the exact interpretation and use of the two ancilla syndromes as one combined syndrome requires some investigation. 

Lastly the currently used ML decoder would benefit from a implementation which is able to avoid the numerical underflow by using a higher precision in its variables.
This could be achieved by reimplementing the algorithm in \texttt{C++} in combination with other discussed optimization approaches.
