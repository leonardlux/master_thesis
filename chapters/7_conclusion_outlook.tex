\chapter{Conclusion}\label{chapter:conclusion}

We started the analysis in this thesis by validating the used implementation. 
This was performed by simulating the QEC setups, including the different decoders and observables, for a code-capacity noise setting,
resulting in thresholds which are consistent with the literature values when taking possible finite-size effects into account.

Regarding the finite-size effects, we observed their occurrence and were able to show
that their impact on the threshold can be reduced by raising the lower bound of the code distance.
With the code distances used in this thesis, we determined thresholds roughly between $0.03\%$ (MWPM) and $0.1\%$ (ML) below the expected literature value.
We concluded that this difference is due to the suppression of the threshold by finite-size effects, 
which also affect all thresholds obtained for the circuit-level noise setting.

Regarding our implementation, we also discussed the importance of decoder choice, showing that 
the maximum likelihood decoder outperforms the minimum weight decoder in regard to the threshold. 

In this process we detected numerical instability in our implementation of the efficient ML decoder,
which lead us to determine the cause and find possible mitigation strategies for future implementations.
The occurrence of those numerical underflow error grows with decreasing physical noise rate and increasing distance. 
By setting an upper limit on the code distance, and by implementing some of the proposed mitigation strategies, 
we were able to perform the threshold analysis with negligible rejection rates.

Turning the focus to the task at hand, the characterization of Steane-type error correction under circuit-level noise,
we found that STEC thresholds are significantly above the thresholds of the conventional syndrome extraction circuit.
Under the assumptions made in this thesis, 
chief among them the assumed access to fault-tolerantly prepared logical encoded ancilla qubits, 
which works in favour of STEC,
we observed thresholds for STEC which are at least a factor $2$ higher than those of the conventional approach. 
 
For the single QEC cycle we determined a threshold at a physical noise rate of approximately $3\%$.
During the analysis we observed the predicted occurrence of an asymmetry in the observable ($\bar{Z}: p_{th} = 2.961 \pm 0.011 \%$ and $\bar{X}: p_{th} = 3.3690 \pm 0.0080 \% $) see section \ref{ssec:results_circ_single_cycle}.
While we are able to qualitatively argue for the occurrence of such an asymmetry, 
quantifying which of the observables is benefiting more from the two opposing mechanism, 
is a task which requires further analysis, as the incoming error model, 
as well as the influence of the residual error decoder has to be taken into account and quantified.

We discussed the influence of the choice of the residual error decoder, 
showing that its choice does not matter for three out of the four configurations, 
while it has a significant influence on the configuration,
which performs ML beforehand and which half-cycle ordering lead to an increased probability of error of the relevant type. 
Giving credence to the asymmetry argumentation before.

The investigation of the choice of decoder during the QEC cycle showed that the ML decoder outperforms the MWPM decoder,
showing a higher threshold for all configurations.
The difference persist even over multiple QEC cycles, stabilizing at approximately $0.1\%$, see table \ref{tab:results_multi_round}.

The overall threshold drops off for the consecutive repeated independent QEC cycles, 
reaching a plateau at $2\%$ after a few rounds, see section \ref{ssec:results_circ_multiple_cycles}. 
We argued that this influence is most likely due to the difference of the incoming error for the consecutive rounds.
While the first cycle obtains a state which is only acted upon by the depolarizing channel applied by the initialization process,
the following cycles are fed with a more complex error model, as the residual error of the previous QEC cycle represents the incoming error.

We also discussed the reduction of the asymmetry between the different observable, 
which we argued is due to the appearing symmetric bulk state, 
as the repeated cycles lead to an constant iterative change between the two half-cycles,
suppressing the asymmetry of the QEC.
We also argued that such an bulk appearance might be broken, if instead of performing a quantum memory experiment,
actual operations would have been performed on the qubits in between, which itself might introduce an asymmetry to the process.

Comparing those results to a conventional repeated syndrome extraction circuit, 
which shows a approximately constant threshold at the physical error rate of close to $1\%$, 
we find that STEC is able to improve the threshold by a factor of $3$ for the single QEC cycle case. 
This improvement drops off to a factor of $2$ for the repeated cycles.

The whole analysis was performed under the assumption that we have access to fault-tolerantly prepared logical encoded qubits, 
with an independent depolarizing error channel on each physical qubit. 
Moreover the ML decoder used was only given the error model of either $X$- or $Z$-type errors. 
Therefore each half-cycle was independently decoded, and the possible information contained in the correlations present in the error channels was neglected.  
Finally we assumed that the conventional syndrome extraction circuit is not decoded independently: 
instead, all syndromes, including the syndrome of the residual error, are decoded together. 

We can conclude the characterization of Steane-type error correction by stating that we were abel to show,
that under circuit-level noise, given our assumptions, 
STEC was able to achieve a threshold which is significantly higher than  
the threshold determined for the conventional syndrome extraction circuit.
The improvement was at least a factor $2$ and even higher for the single QEC round. 

This increased threshold combined with STEC property of constant-in-time scaling of the syndrome extraction process,
make STEC an interesting topic for further research. 

A clear first target for this further research is to reduce the assumptions required in obtaining this threshold, 
which should focus on the major assumption of the access to fault-tolerantly prepared logical encoded ancilla qubits.
As reducing the benefits given to STEC would strengthen the results obtained.
A further outlook is provided in the following section. 

\section{Outlook}

The results of this thesis point toward the benefits of STEC.
Those results would benefit greatly from further investigation into the influence of the fault-tolerant state preparation procedure 
needed for the logical encoded ancilla qubit, as this would remove a major assumption from which STEC is currently benefiting.
Here modeling or implementing a state preparation circuit which is capable of fault tolerantly preparing a logical encoded state 
with a trusted syndrome can be considered a main goal.

On a technical site, the implementation of the maximum likelihood decoder would benefit greatly from a reimplementation in a programming  
language which support higher floating point precision, possibly improving the performance at the same time. 

A potential higher threshold could be achieve for the same experiment performed in this thesis, 
if the correlation between $X$- and $Z$-errors present in the noise model would be used in the decoding process.
Such a decoder is proposed in \cite{efficient_decoding_algorithm}, which implements MLD approximately but still efficiently.
For such an implementation the exact interpretation and use of the two ancilla syndromes as one combined syndrome requires some further investigation. 

The general process of ML decoding would also benefit from an in-depth analysis of the potential uses of the known correlations 
between the data qubit error and the syndrome error model.

Another topic of interest is the analysis of the asymmetry of the STEC circuit.
Here one analyze and quantize this asymmetry in detail, therefore predicting possible differences in the performance with regard to the observable. 
Or related how the asymmetry influences possible operations placed between independent STEC cycles 
and what kind of relation between residual error and incoming error results in the best or worst threshold.
